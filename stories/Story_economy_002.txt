Trump's Russian diesel deal has the US licensing Russian fuel as diesel hits records. Here is what is confirmed, what is disputed, and what comes next.

On Friday 9 October 2026, after a phone call with Vladimir Putin, President Donald Trump announced that Russia would supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel to US and global markets right away, followed by 500,000 tons in November and larger volumes after that. The same day, the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control issued General License 135, which allows transactions in Russian-origin diesel, including imports into the United States, until April 2027. A general license is a Treasury permission that can be withdrawn. It is not a repeal of sanctions, and UK and EU sanctions on Russian oil products remain in force.

On Sunday 11 October, Trump said Russia and Ukraine had agreed an immediate energy ceasefire. That part is disputed. Ukrainian officials said they learned of it from the post, Volodymyr Zelensky said Russian strikes on Ukrainian energy sites continued, and the Kremlin said only that contacts are ongoing. A bipartisan group of US senators has also called on the administration to reverse course, arguing the diesel plan conflicts with a law banning Russian energy imports.

For drivers, the key question is what this does to fuel prices. US diesel averages around $6.26 a gallon according to AAA, well above a year ago. In the UK, the RAC reports a record diesel average near 200p a litre. Analysts quoted in US media say the promised volumes are small compared with US demand, which is opinion rather than fact, and no price or buyer has been announced. Brent crude has been trading around 104 to 108 dollars a barrel, so oil prices and diesel futures remain the numbers to watch.

Still unknown is whether Moscow confirms the truce and the diesel schedule, whether Russia can physically deliver given its own diesel export ban, and whether Congress acts. Next up are the US inflation report on Wednesday 14 October, the Federal Reserve decision on 28 October, the first November diesel tranche, and the US midterm elections on 3 November.
