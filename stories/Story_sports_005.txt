Arteta's new Arsenal contract runs until 2030, and reports say it makes him the Premier League's highest-paid manager. Here is what is confirmed.

Arsenal announced on the morning of 7 October 2026 that Mikel Arteta has signed a new long-term contract that runs to June 2030. His previous deal, signed in September 2024, was due to expire at the end of this season. The club said his coaching staff have renewed too, with those deals subject to regulatory approval.

The headline number is the pay. Sky Sports News, CBS Sports and BBC Sport report that the deal makes the 44-year-old the highest-paid manager in the Premier League, following Pep Guardiola's departure from Manchester City. Arsenal have not published a salary, and the reported figures differ. One report puts it at more than 20 million pounds a season, roughly 26 million dollars, while another describes a base of about 17 million pounds plus bonuses. Treat all of it as reported rather than official.

The reward follows a historic season. Arteta, who joined Arsenal in December 2019, ended the club's 22-year wait for a league title and also took the Gunners to the Champions League final, where they lost to Paris Saint-Germain. Arsenal say he has overseen 361 matches in all competitions with a 62.05 percent win rate, the highest of any permanent manager in the club's history. Co-chairs Stan and Josh Kroenke and chief executive Richard Garlick all backed him, with Garlick saying the club wants to build a winning era together.

Now the focus turns back to the pitch. Arsenal host Leeds United at the Emirates Stadium on Saturday 10 October, then face Lille in the Champions League on Tuesday 13 October. After a 3-0 defeat at Brighton on 19 September, their first league loss of the season, supporters will want to see how the champions respond with their manager secured until 2030.
