The Harry and Meghan Nottingham visit is reportedly set for Thursday, their first joint UK engagement since moving back. Here is what to know before it happens.

Prince Harry and Meghan Markle, the Duke and Duchess of Sussex, are expected to visit Nottingham on Thursday 15 October 2026, according to The Sunday Times, which first reported the plan and cited a friend of the couple. ITV News, AFP and other outlets reported the same itinerary. It would be their first joint engagement since they moved back to the UK in late August after about six years in California. No start times have been published, and neither the Sussexes nor Buckingham Palace had confirmed the plan on the record at the time of writing.

The reported stops are three Nottingham organisations that support young people and local communities: the Community Recording Studio in St Ann's, the Himmah food bank where women from the Hubb Community Kitchen are expected to be involved, and Epic Partners. The visit is not an official royal engagement. Nottingham matters to the couple because it is where they made their first joint appearance after announcing their engagement in December 2017.

The timing is what has people searching. In early September, a letter sent on the King's authority by the Lord Chamberlain, the head of the Royal Household, said the couple's position is "akin to private citizens with commercial and charitable interests". Their spokesperson said they were surprised by it. The Sunday Times said the Nottingham visit signals a determination to be seen as public figures. Whether it changes anything about their status is not known.

Several questions remain open. The exact schedule and who will attend have not been published, and publicly funded police protection for the couple has not been decided, according to ITV News. Nottinghamshire Police will decide what resources, if any, to send. Reports also say the couple plan to spend Christmas in the United States. Until the visit happens or the Sussexes confirm it, everything about the Nottingham plan should be treated as reported and expected, not final.
