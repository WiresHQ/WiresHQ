Bank of America fine: the US Treasury hit the bank with $1.2 billion for money-laundering and sanctions failures, right before earnings. Here is the story.

On Tuesday 13 October 2026, the US Treasury announced more than $1.21 billion in penalties against Bank of America, the second-largest bank in the United States, for violating anti-money-laundering and sanctions laws. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network, known as FinCEN, assessed a $1.2 billion penalty, about £0.91 billion, which Treasury says is the third-largest in its history.

According to Treasury, the bank failed to report more than $5 billion of suspicious transactions on time. More than $1 billion of that activity involved national security risks, including suspected Chinese money-laundering networks and activity linked to the southwest border. Bank of America admitted it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act, the main US law that requires banks to help detect and report money laundering.

Two other agencies were involved. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued a $750 million penalty, the largest in its history, but that amount is credited toward the $1.2 billion total, so the figures should not be added together. The Office of Foreign Assets Control also announced an $11.6 million settlement over 475 apparent sanctions violations. The bank had already been under an OCC cease-and-desist order since December 2024.

For shareholders, the bank said it will book a $1.2 billion charge in its third-quarter results, which reduces net income by about 17 cents per share. The sources reviewed reported no restrictions on customer accounts or deposits. Reactions were mixed, with one analyst calling the one-off charge digestible and a campaign group saying the fine is small compared with the bank's revenue. Bank of America says financial crime compliance is a serious priority and that it has invested billions in its program.
