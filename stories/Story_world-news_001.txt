Pope Leo XIV is set for lung nodule surgery at Gemelli Hospital in Rome. Here is what the Vatican has confirmed so far, and what is still unknown.

Pope Leo XIV, the first American pope, announced the news himself on Sunday 11 October 2026 at the midday Angelus in St Peter's Square. The 71-year-old said that during recent medical checks doctors found a small, well-localised nodule in his lung at a very early stage. Following their advice, he said, he decided to have it surgically removed, and he thanked the faithful for their spiritual closeness.

The Holy See Press Office has since confirmed the practical details. Pope Leo is due to be admitted to Gemelli Hospital in Rome on Tuesday 13 October, and the Vatican says to expect a hospital stay of about one week after the operation. Press office director Matteo Bruni said the nodule was found in routine tests rather than because of a symptom, an infection or inflammation. All of the pope's audiences are cancelled through 20 October, and Cardinal Luis Antonio Tagle, Dean of the College of Cardinals, will preside over Wednesday's closing Mass of the Vatican family conference marking ten years of Amoris Laetitia in his place.

Messages of support have come from around the world. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni expressed her closeness to the pope, Archbishop of Canterbury Sarah Mullally said she is holding him in her prayers, and Cardinal Blase Cupich asked the Archdiocese of Chicago, the pope's home city, to pray for him. Church leaders in the United States and Britain have asked the faithful to pray as well.

Several questions remain open. The exact date and time of the operation has not been confirmed, and the Vatican has not published the size of the nodule, the type of operation or the surgical team. Pope Leo said on Sunday that he still plans to make his trip to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru from 6 to 17 November, but no change to the schedule has been announced either way. Further updates are expected from the Holy See Press Office.
