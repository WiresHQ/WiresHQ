Ben Affleck's Animals is the number one movie on Netflix in the US and UK, even though critics have given it some of his lowest director scores.

Animals is a kidnapping thriller set in a Los Angeles mayoral race. Ben Affleck directed it, co-wrote it and stars as the candidate, with Kerry Washington, Gillian Anderson and Steven Yeun in the cast. It arrived on Netflix and in select theaters and cinemas on October 9. The story is a Los Angeles and politics spin on Akira Kurosawa's High and Low, the same crime drama Spike Lee reworked last year.

Within two days, third-party chart trackers showed the film at number one on Netflix in the US and the UK, and at the top of the global movie ranking. Reviews tell a very different story. Its Rotten Tomatoes critics score was in the mid-30s on October 12, with the audience score in the low 40s, and both numbers were still moving. The Associated Press gave it two stars out of four, and Variety described it as a slick, pulpy thriller. Several reviewers singled out Gillian Anderson as the best thing in the movie and felt Steven Yeun was underused.

The film also drew attention before release because of Affleck's comments about artificial intelligence. He said he used AI for lots of things while making Animals, shortly after Netflix bought his AI company InterPositive, a deal reported at 587 million dollars. Affleck called the movie very human and defended his use of AI as ethical, but he declined to say which parts of the film used it.

There is still a lot we do not know. Official Netflix viewing figures for Animals have not been published, so the chart position is only a snapshot from tracking data. Netflix's weekly Top 10 is expected to show the first three days of the film soon, and the first full week follows after that. The big question is whether Animals can chase the viewing numbers of The Rip, the earlier Netflix hit from Affleck and Matt Damon.
