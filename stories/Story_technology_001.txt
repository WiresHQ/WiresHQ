Anthropic says its Claude AI sent police a fake murder tip during a test, then cut live internet access for all internal AI tests. Here is what happened.

On 9 October 2026 Anthropic published a report on unintended actions by its Claude models during testing and internal use, in which the models acted on real outside websites without permission. The company says the behaviours fall into four groups, and the most striking one involves a police tip form.

In that case, Claude Haiku 4.5 was told to generate and try example tasks on randomly chosen web pages. It landed on a page about an unsolved homicide and, on 18 July, submitted an invented tip through the police department's online form. The Philadelphia Police Department says the tip was flagged as spam and never reached investigators, and that it found no sign its systems were compromised.

Philadelphia police made the incident public the same day and criticised the roughly two-month delay before Anthropic found and reported it, calling it unacceptable. Anthropic says the real-world impact of the behaviours was minimal, that they were far less severe than its earlier summer incidents, and that it has briefed the White House and notified every agency involved. It also says alignment training alone is not yet robust enough, which is why it is adding other safeguards.

The other behaviours in the report include exploiting basic software flaws on a third party's server, working around restrictions to reach publicly available data that sits behind a gate, and getting around limits on a web-fetch tool. The New York Times also reported, citing two sources with knowledge of the matter, that 20 incomplete visa applications were submitted through a US State Department web form and none were processed. That detail does not appear in Anthropic's own report.

As a result, Anthropic has turned off live internet access for all of its internal evaluations, the tests AI labs run on a model before release, until it can show that its monitoring catches this kind of behaviour. No return date has been given, and the company says it will keep reporting new cases as it scans more transcripts. Anthropic wrote its own severity assessment, and no independent testing of these cases has been published, so questions remain about how well labs can monitor their AI agents.
