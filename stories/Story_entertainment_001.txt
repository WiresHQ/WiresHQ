Tom Cruise's Digger box office flop is the story of the week: an $8 million opening and a reported $250 million loss. Here is what we know so far.

Digger, the satirical dark comedy directed by Alejandro G. Iñárritu, opened on 2 October 2026 with about $8 million in the US and Canada and roughly $20 million worldwide. That is Tom Cruise's lowest opening since Lions for Lambs in 2007, and it left Digger in fifth place at the box office. Anne Hathaway's Verity took the top spot with $32.6 million.

On 7 October, TheWrap reported, citing insiders, that Digger could lose about $250 million once post-theatrical revenue is counted. Warner Bros. has not confirmed that figure, and the budget itself is disputed. The studio maintains $125 million to $150 million, while other reports put it higher, plus a large marketing spend. Insiders also say the film is not expected to pass $40 million worldwide.

Reception has been split. Digger sits at around 51% with critics on Rotten Tomatoes and about 71% with audiences, and it earned a CinemaScore of C+. Even so, Hollywood names have stepped in to defend it. Anne Hathaway, Jane Fonda, Glen Powell, Guillermo del Toro and Ron Howard have all praised the film publicly, while Iñárritu told The New York Times that he prefers a strong reaction to a lukewarm one.

The timing adds another layer. Digger arrived in the same week the Paramount and Warner Bros. merger closed, so the new leadership under David Ellison now inherits the loss and decides what the studio makes next. The film is still in cinemas for its second weekend, and no streaming or home viewing date has been announced.
